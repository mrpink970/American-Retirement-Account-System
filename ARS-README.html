American Retirement Account System (ARAS)

A framework for transitioning the retirement portion of Social Security to privately owned, inheritable personal accounts, while preserving disability and survivors benefits as a pooled program and providing a minimum floor for those who outlive their accounts or whose accounts are insufficient.

Why This Exists

Social Security's retirement trust fund is projected to be depleted in 2034. When it is, benefits will be cut automatically by roughly 22% for everyone. The current system has $29.3 trillion in unfunded obligations over 75 years and $71.9 trillion over the infinite horizon, according to the 2026 Social Security Trustees Report.

The system was designed to prevent poverty in old age. It was not designed to make anyone wealthy. It was not designed to pay benefits to millionaires. It was not designed to accumulate $71.9 trillion in unfunded liabilities.

This framework returns Social Security to its original purpose.

What It Does

  • Replaces the retirement portion of Social Security with an account you own
  • Keeps the same 12.4% payroll contribution rate
  • Preserves disability and survivors benefits as a pooled program
  • Provides a minimum floor so no one falls into destitution
  • Caps the private account at $5 million to prevent dynastic wealth accumulation
  • Uses a scaled contribution table so high earners fund the safety net
  • Includes a punitive anti-evasion mechanism for hidden compensation
  • Eliminates the unfunded liability by year 50

Status

Rough draft. Design choices specified. Actuarial modeling needed to calibrate key numbers. Backtesting can be done with available tools to test design resilience.

This framework is a work in progress. It is intended to be scored, critiqued, and revised. The numbers are estimates. The design is the starting point for analysis, not the conclusion.

How to Help

Read the framework. Ask questions. Point out flaws. Share it with your representatives. The framework is done. It needs to be scored, criticized, and improved. That starts with people reading it.

Feedback is welcome. Open an issue or start a discussion. All critiques are useful, whether they support the framework or oppose it.
