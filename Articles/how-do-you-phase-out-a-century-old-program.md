# How Do You Phase Out a Century-Old Program? The ARAS Cohort Schedule

When I tell people that ARAS transitions Social Security into private accounts, the first question they ask is: *"What happens to my parents? What happens to my grandparents? What happens to me?"*

It is the most important question in the entire debate. The 2005 Bush plan failed partly because it terrified current retirees, leaving them unsure if their checks would keep coming. 

The ARAS framework solves this with a strict, transparent age-based phase-in. We do not flip a switch. We do not pull the rug out from under anyone. We use a gradual transition based on your age when the law takes effect. 

Here is exactly how the schedule works, and what it means for your actual retirement check.

### The Rule: Age is Determined on January 1st
To prevent confusion and last-minute gaming of the system, age is determined on January 1st of the year the law takes effect. If your birthday is in December, you are treated as that age on January 1st. 

*(Note: Because the effective date of the law will depend on when Congress passes it, the framework uses age brackets rather than birth years. This ensures the rules remain accurate regardless of the year of enactment.)*

### The Cohorts
Here is how the transition breaks down. Find your age on the start date to see your split:

- **Age 51 and older:** 100% old system, 0% private account. Your benefits are completely untouched. 
- **Age 46–50:** 90% old system, 10% private account. 
- **Age 41–45:** 80% old system, 20% private account.
- **Age 36–40:** 60% old system, 40% private account.
- **Age 31–35:** 40% old system, 60% private account.
- **Age 26–30:** 20% old system, 80% private account.
- **Age 21–25:** 10% old system, 90% private account.
- **Age 20 or younger:** 0% old system, 100% private account.

### 2. Wait, What Do Those Percentages Actually Mean?
If you are 45 years old on the start date, and you fall into the "80% old system, 20% private account" bracket, here is exactly how your paycheck and your retirement work:

**The Contribution (While You Work)**
The total payroll tax is 12.4% (6.2% from you, 6.2% from your employer). Under your cohort's split, **80% of your payroll contribution** goes to the old system to pay legacy benefits, and **20% of your contribution** is routed into the new ARAS system. You don't have to write a check; your employer routes it automatically, just like they do now.

**The Retirement (When You Retire)**
You will receive **two checks**.
- **Check 1 (The Legacy Benefit):** You will get a Social Security check based on your lifetime earnings. However, because you diverted 20% of your contributions into the new system, this legacy check will be *reduced* to reflect that diversion.
- **Check 2 (The ARAS Withdrawal):** You will withdraw money from your personal ARAS private account. Your monthly withdrawal is calculated using an annuity formula designed to last until age 90.

**Your total retirement income is the combination of Check 1 and Check 2.** The goal is for that combined total to be *higher* than what you would have gotten under the old system, because your private account had decades to compound in the market.

### 3. The Legacy Benefit Offset
This is where the math gets tricky, but it is crucial for fairness. We cannot let someone divert 20% of their taxes into a new system *and* receive 100% of their old Social Security benefit. That would bankrupt the system. 

The ARAS framework uses a **Legacy Benefit Offset**. When you retire, the Social Security Administration calculates what your old benefit *would have been*. Then, they reduce it by the value of the taxes you diverted.

For example: Let's say your old benefit would have been $2,000 a month. Because you diverted 20% of your contributions, your new legacy check might be $1,600 a month. But your private account (thanks to decades of compounding) might generate an additional $800 a month. Your total income is now $2,400—higher than the old system would have given you.

### 4. The End of the "Cliff" Retirement (And the Government Telling You What to Do)
Under the current system, retirement is a punitive cliff. You either work full-time until your Full Retirement Age, or the government permanently cuts your check by 30% if you claim at 62. And if you try to work part-time, the Earnings Test withholds even more of your money.

ARAS changes this. Because you have two separate pools of money, you have control. You can work full-time, part-time, or not at all. The government no longer penalizes you for staying in the workforce.

**If you are a Transition Cohort (you have a legacy benefit):**
Imagine you are 62 and want to drop down to part-time work. Under ARAS:
1. **You tap your ARAS account at 62.** The ARAS account has no earnings test and no penalty for working. You start receiving your monthly annuity payment (calculated to last until age 90) to supplement your part-time income. You do not have the option to withdraw the entire balance at once—the annuity schedule protects you from outliving your money.
2. **You delay your legacy Social Security check.** Because your ARAS annuity is covering your bills, you don't need to claim your traditional benefit yet. You wait until 70. 
3. **You max out your legacy benefit.** By waiting until 70, your traditional Social Security check gets the maximum delayed retirement credit.

*(Note: If you are working full-time at 62 and don't need the ARAS money, the smart financial move is to not claim it yet. Leave it in the account to keep growing so your annuity is bigger when you do claim it.)*

**If you are 20 or Under (100% ARAS):**
You won't have a legacy check to delay. But you still get the freedom. At 62, you can drop to part-time work, keep working full-time, or stop entirely. If you claim your ARAS annuity while working, there is no Earnings Test and no penalty. You aren't forced into a binary choice between full-time work and full retirement; you control your own timeline.

**Doesn't this add cost to the system? No.** 
Critics will argue that letting transition cohorts tap their accounts at 62 while delaying their legacy benefits will cost more. It won't. If that worker chose to work until 70 under the current system, they would get the exact same maximized legacy benefit. ARAS doesn't add a cost; it simply gives the worker the financial freedom to choose their own retirement timeline without being punished by the government. 

### 5. The "20 and Under" Crowd: Why You Are Actually the Winners

If you are 20 or younger, you might look at this chart and think: *"Wait, the 50-year-olds get a guaranteed government check, but I get all the market exposure? That's not fair!"*

I understand why it feels that way. But here is the brutal reality: under this framework, your generation is getting the best deal of anyone in the country. 

**The Current System is Leaving You Nothing**
The Social Security Trust Fund is projected to be depleted in roughly a decade. If Congress does nothing, your generation is facing an automatic 22% cut to your benefits. But we all know Congress won't do nothing. They will "fix" it—either by raising the retirement age, increasing taxes on the middle class, or cutting benefits in some other way. All of those options just kick the can down the road again. You are paying 12.4% of your paycheck for a promise that keeps getting worse. 

**You Get Maximum Ownership**
The 50-year-old gets a guaranteed check, but they are out of time. They only have 12 years to build wealth. You have at least 42 years of compounding ahead of you. Under ARAS, your cohort gets the largest private account share of anyone in the system. The vast majority of your retirement contribution goes into an account with your name on it. It is not a government IOU. It is your property. 

**You Have the Power of Compounding (and the Freedom to Keep Going)**
You have the single most powerful advantage in finance: time. A 40+ year horizon in a diversified index fund is the greatest wealth-building engine ever created. The 50-year-old is getting a guaranteed check because they don't have the time to let compound interest work for them. You do. And because ARAS doesn't force you to retire at a certain age, you can keep working and keep contributing as long as you want, growing your account until you hit the $5 million cap. More time, more growth, less government telling you what to do.

**The Current System Fails at Generational Wealth**
Under the current system, if you die at 61, your 40 years of paying into Social Security vanish. Your children are likely adults by now, which means they don't even qualify for a survivor check. The wealth you built is gone. It does not pass to your family. 

**ARAS Builds Generational Wealth**
Under ARAS, if you die at 61, your adult children inherit the entire balance of your private account. They get a one-time election: they can roll all or some of the funds into their own ARAS account completely free, or they can cash out some or all of it (subject to a 10% penalty if they are under 62, which goes back into the disability and floor pools). 

The 50-year-old is getting a guaranteed check because they are out of time. You are getting an inheritable, growing asset because you have the one thing they don't: decades of compound interest. 

### 6. How Does the Withdrawal Actually Work? (And Where Does the Money Go?)

If you are 45 today, you have spent decades watching your ARAS account grow in the market. But what happens when you actually decide to claim the money? Does it just sit in cash? Do you lose the growth?

Here is exactly how the mechanics work:

**1. The Annuity Formula Determines the Amount**
You don't get to withdraw the entire balance at once. When you choose to begin drawing from your account (whether that is at 62, 70, or 85), the ARAS board uses a standard annuity formula. It calculates your monthly check based on your current balance, your remaining life expectancy (designed to deplete at age 90), and an assumed rate of return. 

**2. The 100% Bond Switch**
The moment you start drawing your money, the game changes. You are no longer trying to maximize growth; you are trying to protect what you have. Therefore, the framework mandates that your entire account is immediately shifted to **100% government securities (the G Fund)**. 

This is a hard rule. Once withdrawals begin, you cannot go back to the stock market. This completely eliminates the risk of a market crash wiping out your retirement the day after you claim your benefits. 

**3. The Money Keeps Growing (Safely)**
Even though you are now 100% in government bonds, the money that you haven't withdrawn yet **does not just sit there**. It continues to earn safe, guaranteed interest. 
Because your remaining balance is still earning interest (and because your life expectancy gets shorter every year), the annuity formula **recalculates your monthly check every single year**. Your check is adjusted based on the actual returns of the G Fund. If the account earns exactly what was projected, your check stays stable. If it earns more, your check goes up. 

### 7. What if I Live Past 90? Or What if My Account is Too Small?

This is the most important safety net in the entire framework, and it is the part that guarantees ARAS is not just a system for the rich. 

The annuity formula is designed to deplete your account at age 90. But what if you live to 95? What if you were a low-wage earner your whole life, and your account simply isn't big enough to cover your bills? What if you were 20 when ARAS started, meaning you don't have a legacy Social Security check to fall back on?

You will not be left with nothing. 

**1. The 28% Floor (No Legacy Check Required)**
ARAS includes a strict Minimum Floor that is completely independent of the old Social Security system. If your private account is depleted, or if your monthly annuity check is too small to live on, the Floor Reserve Pool kicks in. 

The floor is set at 28% of the National Average Wage Index. It is not a life of luxury, but it is a guarantee that you will not fall into destitution. You will be able to afford food, rent, and basic necessities, regardless of whether you ever received a traditional Social Security check.

**2. How the Means Test Works (No Subsidies for the Rich)**
The Floor Reserve is a safety net, not a universal handout. You cannot collect it if you have substantial wealth or income. 

To qualify, you must meet **both** of the following conditions:
- **Income Test:** Your total monthly retirement income (from your ARAS annuity, any legacy Social Security, or other pensions) must fall below the floor threshold.
- **Asset Test:** Your liquid assets (cash, stocks, bonds—excluding your primary home) must be below a set limit. 

If you are a high earner with a $100,000 retirement income, or if you have a million dollars sitting in a brokerage account, you do not get a dime from the floor pool. It is strictly reserved for people whose total retirement income and savings have run out.

**3. 24/7 Transparency and Control**
Because ARAS is your money, you have complete visibility. Every account holder has secure, 24/7 web and mobile access to their balance, their contribution history, and their projected monthly retirement income. 

This transparency is crucial. If you are 85 and you see that your account is projected to run out at 90, you and your family have the freedom to adjust your spending and save a little more. The government is not hiding your money from you; you are in the driver's seat.

**4. What if I Outlive My Account?**
If you live past 90, your ARAS account will be fully depleted. At that point, if your total income and liquid assets fall below the threshold, the Floor Reserve will supplement your income to keep you above the poverty floor. You will never be left with zero, but you must qualify for the safety net.
